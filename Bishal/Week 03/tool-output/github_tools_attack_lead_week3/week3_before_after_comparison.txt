This week also included threshold tuning to compare how different settings affected alert behaviour.
 For the SYN scan rule, I first tested a lower threshold, which made the rule more sensitive and allowed scan activity to be detected quickly.
 After that, I increased the threshold to reduce alert noise and see whether the rule would still work.
 In my testing, the scan still triggered alerts even after the threshold was increased because the nmap -sS -p- scan generated enough SYN packets to cross the stricter threshold.
 This showed that the scan activity in the lab was strong enough to remain visible even when the rule became less sensitive.

The tuning process showed that increasing the threshold can help reduce unnecessary alerts, but it also creates a risk of missing smaller or slower scans.
In this lab, the stricter setting still detected the full-port SYN scan, so the difference in alert count was not very large.
Even so, in a real environment, setting the threshold too high could reduce recall because a slower or more careful attacker may avoid detection.
This week showed that tuning is about finding a balance between sensitivity and noise, not just making the rule stricter.
